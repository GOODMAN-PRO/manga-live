# 00 — Pitch

Three premises. Same fusion brief: Wistoria's magicless swordsman and his tower,
SAO's anonymous handles and real stakes, Horimiya's engine — two people who only
know each other's real self, learning each other in daylight.

---

## Candidate 1 — **The Seed** *(showrunner)*

Tōka Academy's tower is dived, not entered: students go in as generated figures
under coined handles, identities sealed by regulation so the rankings can't be
politicked. NULL and KETTLE have held the front line together for a year — the
two most feared divers in the building, and neither has ever seen the other's
face. Outside, KETTLE is Ariake Kanade, ranked first in the year, gracious,
immaculate, universally admired and close to nobody. NULL is Hazama Kou, the
magicless boy his class calls the blank pin, who absorbs whatever they throw and
says *yeah, fair*, and goes home. They have exchanged perhaps eleven sentences in
daylight in two years.

**Gut-punch it enables:** the only place either of them is a real person is a
place that is charging them, floor by floor, the exact capacity they'd need to
have an ordinary life together.

---

## Rival 2 — **The Ledger**

The tower takes memory as toll — one chosen memory per layer cleared, surrendered
at the gate. Front-liners are people who have deliberately spent their pasts, and
the highest climbers are strangers to their own families. Our two have fought
together for a year and neither can now remember why they started. The romance
runs on a fuse: they are falling for each other while burning the shared history
that would let them recognise each other anywhere.

**Gut-punch it enables:** he realises he has already paid her face once, and
bought it back, and doesn't know how many times.

---

## Rival 3 — **Understudies**

Dives are broadcast; front-liners are celebrities. The two best are anonymous
because both are cheating the system in opposite directions — he has no magic and
is not legally permitted inside; she is ranked first and is *forbidden* to dive
because she's too valuable to risk. Both handles exist because both of them are
breaking a rule. Discovery isn't "oh, it's you." It's: we are now each other's
leverage, and the only safe place either of us has is the other one's silence.

**Gut-punch it enables:** the first thing they ever hold together is a threat,
and it takes a year to turn into custody.

---

## Decision: **Candidate 1, with Rival 3's rule-breaking grafted in and Rival 2's cost discipline**

The Ledger erases what Horimiya needs to accumulate — a romance built on
forgetting can't do domesticity — and Understudies is a thriller premise wearing
a romcom coat. The seed wins because anonymity-by-regulation is the cleanest
possible reason two people can trust each other completely for a year and still
walk past each other at breakfast, and because it lets the discovery land in
Ch.1–2 as a *beginning*, exactly as Horimiya does. From Rival 3 I take that both
of them are in breach — he is diving illegally on a nullified pin, she is diving
against a faculty ban — so the discovery hands each of them a weapon and neither
picks it up; from Rival 2 I take the discipline of a toll that is chosen,
concrete and cumulative, and I make it sensation rather than memory, because a
tower that charges you your sense of touch is charging you the currency this
particular love story is denominated in.
