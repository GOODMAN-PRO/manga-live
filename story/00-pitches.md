# 00 — Premise Pitches

Three candidates. Same engine (two people whose school faces are lies, one private
night world where the lies come off), three different rooms to put it in.

---

## Candidate 1 — **The Bathhouse** *(showrunner seed)*

Shirakawa Nagi is first on every exam list and nobody in class has ever seen her
face move. What nobody knows is that at 6pm she walks into a half-shuttered
shopping street and opens her dead grandfather's tiny sentō, alone: lights the
boiler, scrubs the tile, sells bottled milk to four old regulars, closes at
11:30, and does her homework at the counter with her feet in the drain channel.
Nomura Itsuki is the loudest boy in class, the one who makes school bearable for
everyone else, and the eldest of three children whose mother works nights — he
cooks, bathes his brother, and has not been tired *at* anyone in four years.
He finds her at midnight in the rain, because on a dead street a bathhouse is
the only thing still lit.

**Gut-punch it enables:** a girl who runs the one place in town whose entire
purpose is to make tired people warm — and no one has asked her, in fourteen
months, whether *she* is tired. When someone finally does, she doesn't have an
answer ready, and that's what breaks her.

---

## Candidate 2 — **The 2AM Laundry**

Class president, blazer always immaculate, never once wrinkled — because she
washes and presses her uniform at one in the morning in a 24-hour coin laundry
two stations from where she's supposed to live. Her house is not a place she can
be in; she doesn't say why and the story doesn't rush her. The boy who works the
graveyard shift at the conbini next door is the class's cheerful idiot, and he
notices her because the dryer she uses is the broken one and he's the one who
kicks it. Their private world is fluorescent, humming, and smells like detergent:
two folding tables, a vending machine, and four hours nobody else wants.

**Gut-punch it enables:** the moment he realises the crispness everyone admires
at school is *maintenance* — that being flawless is a night job — and instead of
pitying her he just starts kicking the dryer before she asks.

---

## Candidate 3 — **Signal**

At 1:04am a pirate radio station broadcasts for about forty minutes on a
frequency between two dead ones. The host calls herself Nagi-nashi and says
things nobody says out loud. Itsuki has listened every night for eleven months
because it's the only voice in his life that wants nothing from him. He works
out who she is not from anything she confesses, but from a sound: at 1:22 the
level crossing behind the shotengai rings in her background, and he's standing
at that crossing. Their private world is a school broadcast room, a stolen
transmitter, and two people who have already told each other everything while
believing they were strangers.

**Gut-punch it enables:** he has to decide whether to knock — because the second
she knows he knows, the one place she can be honest stops being safe.

---

## Decision: **Candidate 1 — The Bathhouse**

Radio is one-directional and the laundry is thin on stakes and cast, but a sentō
puts both leads in the same room doing small physical work, and small physical
work performed in silence is the entire engine Horimiya runs on. It is also
literally the building where masks come off: no uniform, no makeup, steam that
hides and reveals on the artist's schedule, and an aging shopping street that
supplies a warm ensemble and a slow, bureaucratic closure clock instead of plot
machinery. The best pieces of the losing pitches survive as grafts — recognition
by *sound* rather than by confession (Ch.6, Ch.13), and "flawlessness is a night
job" as Nagi's cracked, hidden hands, planted in Ch.1 page 5 and paid off on
page 15.
