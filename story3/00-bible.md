# 借り物競走 — KARIMONO
### *"The Borrowed-Item Race"*
**Series bible v1 — LOCKED. Read `FORMAT.md` and `art-direction.md` alongside this.**

---

## LOGLINE

Kusaka Hibiki can borrow one other person's ability for exactly sixty seconds — but only from someone he has touched that day, and only once each. Alone he is the single weakest student at Tōka High. On a team he is the most versatile racer alive, because he is *everyone*. He joins a school club whose sport is a scavenger race through a monster-filled Pit, and falls for the one person in the building who will not let him touch her.

**Tone: LOUD. BRIGHT. FUNNY.** Crowds, rankings, sponsors, announcers, rival schools, a club room that smells like feet. Comedy first. The feeling sneaks in underneath and ambushes you.

**One-line pitch:** *a sports manga where the sport is dungeon-diving and the hero's power is other people.*

## THE LAW OF THE BORROW

These eleven rules are the physics of the series. **They are never broken, never upgraded, never "unlocked."** Every fight is a puzzle solved *inside* them. If a chapter ever needs a rule bent, the chapter is wrong.

| # | LAW |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Skin to skin.** Bare contact, however brief — handshake, high-five, shoulder grab, a finger on a wrist. Through cloth, hair, glove or sleeve: **nothing.** |
| **2** | **Today only.** The touch must have happened since midnight. He sleeps, the slate wipes. |
| **3** | **Once per person per day.** Touching someone a second time gives him nothing new. He gets one pull from each person, ever, per day. |
| **4** | **Exactly sixty seconds.** Not fifty-nine, not sixty-one. No pausing, no extending, no early return. He counts out loud. Everyone counts with him. |
| **5** | **One at a time.** Starting a new borrow instantly kills the current one. There is no stacking. |
| **6** | **He gets the ability, not the skill.** Gaku's arms stretch — Hibiki still has to aim, and he is bad at aiming. Borrowed power in untrained hands is a *liability* as often as an edge. |
| **7** | **The lender loses nothing.** They don't feel it. They don't know. There is no drain, no cost, no cooldown on their side. |
| **8** | **He has no ability of his own.** "Borrow" is not a power — it is what he *is*. Measured cold, alone, in a booth, he scores zero. The school's records list him as **Null**. |
| **9** | **No self, no beasts, no dead.** He cannot borrow from himself, from Fauna, or from a body. |
| **10** | **No lending on.** What he borrows is his alone. He cannot pass it, share it, or hold it in reserve. |
| **11** | **THE CARD.** The instant he touches someone, the *name* of their ability appears in his head — a plain white card with a few words on it. He always knows what he could take. He does not know what it does, what it costs, or how to use it. **This makes him the best scout in Japan and the worst fighter.** |

**Corollaries the reader learns by watching, not by being told:**
- He touches *constantly*. Handshakes, high-fives, palm to the back of a neck. It reads as friendliness because it mostly is.
- Sixty seconds is a long time in a conversation and no time at all in a fight.
- His real power is **the roster** — who is in the building, whose hand he has already shaken today, and who he has been saving.
- His real weakness is **the morning**. At 8am he is nobody.

## THE SPORT

**Pits** are geological anomalies under Japanese cities: cavern systems that keep going, full of **Fauna**. Two generations ago they were a disaster. Now they are mapped, fenced, floodlit, insured, sponsored, and — the way rivers became rowing — turned into a school sport.

**The Karimono (借り物競走)** is that sport. It is a scavenger race.

1. The drum is spun. A sealed card comes out. The announcer reads it aloud to the stands.
2. The card names a thing: *"a bell that has never rung." "Something older than this school." "Two of the same." "A light that isn't yours."*
3. Two six-person teams go into the Pit at the horn.
4. First team back across the line holding something that satisfies the card wins the **leg**.
5. The **Judge** rules on whether the item counts. The Judge is final. The Judge is also, frequently, an idiot.
6. Best of three legs takes the meet.

**Why it's a great sport:** the card is random, so nobody can prepare a play; the Pit fights back; and half the drama is *argument* — whether a thing is the thing.

**Fauna tiers:** **Litter** (small, swarming, dumb) → **Bulk** (big, slow, load-bearing) → **Warden** (one per Pit, territorial, does not leave its floor, will absolutely end a high schooler's season).

**Governing body:** JHPA. National ladder, prefectural qualifiers, a summer tournament, rank badges on the collar, and a magazine that ranks first-years and ruins their lives.

## THE TWO LEADS

### KUSAKA HIBIKI 日下 響 — first year
**Ability: BORROW.** Sixty seconds. See The Law.

**Visual lock (greyscale):** Short — the shortest boy in the club, and it never stops being funny. Compact, wiry, no bulk. **Hair: solid black, large, clumped into sharp wedge tips that stick straight up and forward like something exploded behind him — the darkest mass on any page he's in. Hard-edged white highlight bands, no grey.** Round, wide-open eyes, large iris, huge catchlight, thick upper lash line — a *bright* face, permanently mid-sentence. Small mouth that is almost always open. One bandage on the bridge of his nose, replaced daily, never explained.

**Outfit:** Tōka blazer worn open over an untucked shirt, tie loose and crooked, sleeves shoved to the elbow — **forearms and hands always bare, always visible.** Club jersey (dark, white piping, big pale number **0** on the back) over the shirt on race days. Sneakers with the heels crushed flat because he can't be bothered.

**Silhouette test:** small + enormous spiked black head + both palms open and forward.

**Voice:** loud, cheerful, zero shame, asks devastating questions by accident. Uses everyone's first name within four seconds of meeting them. Says "**can I—**" and is already reaching.

**The engine:** he is not sad about being Null. He genuinely thinks other people's abilities are the coolest thing in the world and he wants to *try them all*. His flaw isn't self-pity, it's that he has never once in his life had to be only himself — and he's not sure there's anyone in there.

---

### ANZAI SUMIRE 安西 菫 — first year, ace of the Karimono Club
**Ability: DECLARE (宣言).** She says out loud, precisely, what she is about to do. If she then does it *exactly* as declared — no deviation, no improvisation, no correction — it is unstoppable, and nothing in the Pit can prevent it. If she deviates by a hair, it fails **completely** and she is left standing there having announced her plan to everyone.

She is the ace because she never deviates. She is terrifying because she tells you first.

**Visual lock (greyscale):** Tall for a first-year, a full head over Hibiki. Long-limbed, athletic, straight-backed. **Hair: near-white — rendered as blown-out white with thin contour line and only a whisper of tone, the exact photographic negative of Hibiki's solid black.** Worn in a high ponytail, clumped in heavy masses with sharp wedge tips that read as a single blade shape from behind. Straight blunt fringe. Eyes: pale iris with a light-to-lighter gradient, small dark pupil, single hard catchlight — cool, level, unimpressed.

**Outfit — the tell:** high-collared shirt buttoned to the throat. Club jersey worn **open** over it, sleeves long and pulled down past the wrists, hooked over her thumbs. Athletic tape wound over the backs of her hands. Below the jaw, **you never see her skin.** Not once, not in summer, not in the bath episode, not until the series says so.

**Silhouette test:** tall vertical + white blade of ponytail + two sleeve-covered hands hanging at her sides.

**Voice:** flat, declarative, unexpectedly funny because she does not know she's being funny. Announces her intentions in public with no embarrassment whatsoever — which is *its own* comedy, because the mechanic forces her to shout things like "I AM GOING TO STEP ON HIS FACE."

**The engine:** she does not let anyone touch her, she will not discuss why, and she has never explained it to a single person in the club. So the boy who is only ever other people has fallen for the only person he can never be. **He can borrow anything in Japan except her.** That is the romance and it is also the plot; they are the same machine.

**Her rule mirrors his.** He can only be someone else. She can only ever be exactly what she said she'd be. Neither of them gets to improvise.

## TONE RULES

1. **A laugh every page, a joke every second page, a big one every five.** If a page has no comedy in it, it had better be page 22 of 25.
2. **The comedy is in the mechanics.** The best jokes come from the Law: he borrowed the wrong guy; the sixty seconds ran out mid-air; she had to declare something humiliating in front of a crowd.
3. **Never sad about the power.** Hibiki thinks being Null is a *scheduling problem*.
4. **Crowd noise is a character.** Bleachers, announcers, banners, a school band that is not good. Use `crowd` and `announcer` as speakers freely.
5. **Sincerity is short and ambushes you.** One page, sometimes one panel. Then someone says something stupid and we're out. Never linger; the register switch (chibi ↔ rendered) is the timing device.
6. **Every chapter has a fight, a contest, or a physical set piece.** Even the ones about a melon.
7. **Every chapter ends on a hook.** A challenge, an arrival, a card, or a line.
8. **Episodic first.** A reader picking up chapter 47 should be fine by page 4.
9. **New person = new toy.** Every chapter can introduce a new ability. The well is infinite; use it.
10. **No confession before ch.40.** See below.

## 100-CHAPTER ARC MAP

### ARC 1 — THE NINE (ch. 1–10)

## PRODUCTION NOTES FOR THE ART PIPELINE

- **Hibiki is the darkest mass on every page.** Solid black hair, hard white highlight bands, no grey. If a page reads flat, he's not black enough.
- **Sumire is the brightest.** Blown-out white hair with contour line only. The two of them in one panel = the series' entire graphic identity.
- **Chibi register carries the comedy** and is used *constantly* — it's a beat, not a scene. A rendered panel next to a chibi panel is the house joke.
- **Silhouette panels are pivots**, 1–4 a chapter, and for Sumire they do double duty: a covered figure with no visible skin is *already* halfway to a silhouette.
- **Crowds are tone, not linework.** Bleachers = mass + a few faces at the front.
- **Pit interiors** are tone-washed with two or three hard perspective lines. Full architecture only for the establishing shot of a new Pit floor.
- **The sixty-second count is a visual motif.** Numbers as SFX in the panel corner — `59` `41` `12` `3` — big, blunt, and always accurate.
